Wilkes-Barre & Back Mountain Venues to Know

From a historic mansion to barns and garden pavilions, with planning notes for each style.

The Stegmaier

156 S. Franklin Street, Wilkes-Barre

  • Two historic properties: the Mary Stegmaier Mansion (built 1910) and the Frederick Stegmaier guesthouse (built 1870)
  • Grand ballroom for ceremonies and receptions, with up to 120 guests for a reception and a full-mansion buyout for larger celebrations
  • Guest rooms at the Frederick Stegmaier guesthouse

Planner’s note: Historic mansions reward careful flow planning: where guests gather between ceremony and reception, where the wedding party gets ready, and when vendors can load in.

Details from The Stegmaier; confirm current packages, capacity and policies with the venue.

The Beaumont Inn

Route 309, Dallas (Back Mountain)

  • 50 acres of botanical gardens for ceremonies and photos
  • Climate-controlled Grande Pavilion for up to 300 guests, usable year-round
  • Two-tiered flagstone patios overlooking Leonard’s Creek, plus on-site wedding specialists and overnight rooms

Planner’s note: When the wedding party stays on site, getting-ready, first look and photo timing can be built around the gardens without travel time. Plan an indoor or covered backup for an outdoor ceremony.

Details from The Beaumont Inn; confirm current packages, capacity and policies with the venue.

Irem Pavilion

70 Ridgway Drive, Dallas

  • Outdoor venue on the Irem Country Club grounds, surrounded by an 18-hole golf course
  • Ceremonies beneath a wedding arch on the course, cocktail hour on the pavilion deck
  • Reception in the main pavilion, rain or shine, with a liberal decoration policy

Planner’s note: Open-air pavilions are a blank canvas: decor, lighting, rentals and an evening temperature plan (layers, heaters, bug spray) should all be on the checklist.

Details from Irem Pavilion; confirm current packages, capacity and policies with the venue.

The AppleTree Terrace at Newberry Estate

Newberry Estate, Dallas

  • Outdoor ceremony site with a pergola, plus an indoor option
  • Large indoor banquet hall with an attached bar room and patio
  • Seating for 180 to 200 guests; Friday, Sunday and winter wedding packages at discounted prices

Planner’s note: An outdoor ceremony with a ready indoor fallback is ideal. Decide the weather call time with the venue in advance so vendors and guests aren’t left guessing.

Details from The AppleTree Terrace; confirm current packages, capacity and policies with the venue.

The Barn at Shadyrill Farm

Loyalville Road, Dallas

  • A family home and working produce farm for 45 years
  • Renovated barn with rough-cut lumber walls, chandeliers and 15-foot ceilings
  • Two restrooms, a bridal room and a large kitchen for catering setup

Planner’s note: Barn weddings usually mean bringing in your own caterer and rentals. A coordinator keeps delivery windows, setup, breakdown and vendor meals on one schedule.

Details from The Barn at Shadyrill Farm; confirm current packages, capacity and policies with the venue.

Friedman Farms

Dallas

  • Hosting weddings since 2006 on 145 acres of fields and hilltop views
  • A climate-controlled, 140-foot, three-level barn seating up to 200 guests
  • Outdoor ceremony sites including a hilltop, a fireplace and a gazebo, plus an indoor loft

Planner’s note: Multiple ceremony sites mean choosing one early and planning a guest path, seating and sound for it, along with a rain plan.

Tips for a Wilkes-Barre or Back Mountain Wedding

  • Barn and farm venues often need outside catering, rentals and lighting; budget for them and confirm delivery and pickup windows.
  • For outdoor pavilions and gardens, plan for evening temperatures, bugs and lighting after sunset.
  • Venues with on-site rooms make getting ready, first looks and photos much easier, so ask about them.
  • Ask every venue what’s included and what you bring in (catering, rentals, bar, decor), plus its rules on outside vendors, candles and end times.
  • Have a rain plan for any outdoor ceremony, and agree on when the weather call will be made.
  • Build buffer time into the timeline for photos, travel between ceremony and reception, and hair and makeup.
  • Share one master timeline and vendor contact sheet with every vendor in the final weeks.
